Problem. Plant tissue culture laboratories constantly lose cultures to bacterial and fungal contamination. It damages plantlets and can wipe out whole batches. The early stage cannot be seen by the unaided eye or caught in routine visual inspection, so labs usually find it two to three days after it starts, when it may already have spread to other vessels. In most laboratories, 3–15% of plants are lost to contamination at every subculture (Leifert and Waites, 1994).

Proposed solution. TissueTrack is a software platform that analyses the numerical data from GC-IMS (gas chromatography–ion mobility spectrometry) to detect unusual patterns in volatile organic compounds (VOCs) that may indicate microbial contamination. Its AI compares each reading with normal culture patterns and flags abnormalities. A fixed camera adds a photo of every tube and jar each 30–60 minutes, so the system can also track small visible changes over time. All analysis runs on the lab server, and the researcher receives only the result on their phone: the vessel, its status, a risk score and the latest photo.

Ground-level applicability and impact. TissueTrack uses a GC-IMS instrument the lab already has, a low-cost camera and one server, so a lab can adopt it without changing how it grows cultures. Researchers check only the flagged vessels and can isolate them early. This could help labs identify contamination sooner, save valuable cultures and materials, and spend less time investigating losses. In future, the same approach could extend to other cell cultures and fermentation processes.
